**Assignment:** Continue `starfield-resource-research` after SRR-006. Produce a qualified, reviewable path from the existing four-site hardware observer to an *explicitly authorized future* no-save retail observation. **This brief authorizes offline engineering, read-only inspection, and disposable executable tests only. It does not authorize launching or attaching to Starfield, changing Steam/game settings, deploying SFSE, loading saves, or carrying out Stage A.**

## Controlling question

Can the SRR-006 observer be configured, integrated, and independently qualified for a bounded Stage A observation of the pinned retail Starfield build, with honest coverage boundaries and controlled capture-first retirement? If process-start coverage is not achievable under the actual Steam launch model, establish that limitation, qualify the best safe alternative within offline authority, and state precisely what further live authority or engineering is needed. **Do not claim startup coverage on the basis of a Steam URI launch followed by later attachment.**

## Engineering scope

1. **Assess the actual launch/observation boundary.** Map the distinction between (a) the Steam URI operation, (b) the process that ultimately hosts the pinned game image, (c) an observer-controlled creation event, and (d) a later attach barrier. Identify what the present SRR-006 controller supports, what a proposed retail adapter would add, and how exact process identity, creation epoch, image hash, module map, thread census and hardware-slot readback are verified. Give explicit PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN gates for each approach. Use real Windows documentation and existing local prior art where helpful. A Steam URI yielding an opaque game PID must not be represented as pre-instruction coverage. If a robust startup route is not demonstrable offline, make the post-title attach candidate a *separate, qualified proposal*, preserving the uncovered interval and possibility that module-state construction already occurred.
2. **Implement only justified, bounded integration.** Prepare the relevant launch/attach adapter, configuration validation, operator marker channel and evidence plumbing where an offline, reproducible test can establish their behavior. Keep any retail entry point unable to act on the game without a separately reviewed, explicit live-admission gate. Reuse the four-site profile, verified code-window/relocation predicates, hardware ownership, per-thread coverage model, causal joins and durable journal from SRR-006. Reject wrong process, unexpected handoff, changed executable, wrong module/system fingerprint, changed debugger ownership or incomplete arming. Preserve useful evidence from incomplete sessions without upgrading missing events to a negative result. Do not weaken safety checks merely to make launch possible.
3. **Title-card/operator workflow.** Model `STARTUP`, `TITLE_CARD`, `MAIN_MENU`, `STOP`, `RETIRE`, and `EXIT` as distinct, timestamped states/markers. The operator confirms the title card, presses a key/button to advance, confirms the responsive main menu, and later uses the game's Quit action. Codex/controller may start the game only in a future explicitly authorized live assignment; Codex must not automate game UI interactions. Markers are operator statements tied to the authenticated run/PID and receipt sequence, not native timestamps or proof of an engine event. There is no save load in Stage A. No Steam-overlay screenshots are required.
4. **Capture-first and anomaly policy.** `STOP` closes *scientific admission*, drains and flushes a verifiable journal prefix, and records what was and was not observed; `RETIRE` and process `EXIT` are separate engineering outcomes. Valid, authenticated pre-STOP observations remain usable if the game subsequently crashes during approved retirement (the historical “kamikaze trace” pattern). Do not intentionally crash or terminate the target unless a future approval explicitly permits a specific recovery action. Anomalous but safely observable callbacks, modules, return paths or game delays are evidence: classify and continue *within the approved capture scope*. Invalidate only affected coverage where necessary. Stop/contain actual integrity, identity, storage, control or safety failures; never treat a strange scientific signal as an automatic reason to shut down (“shy SETI telescope”). No unapproved widening of breakpoints or memory probes.
5. **Time and health.** No arbitrary experiment-length, idle-time, no-hit, event-count, or delayed-operator abort. Heavy breakpoint traffic may slow Starfield greatly. OS-call polling intervals and disposable-test watchdogs are not production science deadlines. Monitor real event-pump progress, known event ownership, queue/writer health, disk space and operator-directed STOP. Provide a bounded, separately authorized emergency procedure for a genuinely unresponsive controller or target; normal slowness alone is not such a condition. A missed monitoring cadence is recorded, not automatically fatal. Maintain the free-space floor of **40,000,000,000 caller-available bytes on each of C: and D:** after projected material writes.
6. **Isolation, save protection and rights.** Stage A's proposed environment is no-save, SFSE absent, Steam/NVIDIA overlays disabled, Steam Cloud disabled and independently qualified empty/isolated writable saves. These are *future run-card requirements*, not authority to alter settings now. Preserve active saves, independent backups and Steam sync state; maintain exact cleanup and restoration responsibilities. Report module approval/unknown-module behavior without treating every unfamiliar but harmless module as a fatal scientific anomaly. A module change that breaks the validated coverage model must explicitly invalidate that window. No proprietary binaries, raw native payloads or private paths in public deliverables.
7. **Offline qualification.** Build/reuse isolated disposable Win32 fixtures to test the selected adapter and its refusal gates: startup identity, true initial-thread arming versus later attach, process handoff, ASLR, newly created workers, four simultaneous sites, title/menu/STOP markers, unknown module load/unload, delayed operator transitions, anomalous callbacks, per-window coverage loss, queue/writer failure, durable pre-STOP evidence after failed retirement, and exact-child emergency refusal/recovery. Test the *actual integrated entry point*, not only helper functions. Record untested conditions separately (notably Steam/game behavior). Do not conduct a live retail smoke test under this assignment.
